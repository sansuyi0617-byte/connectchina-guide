China eSIM: what to prepare before your trip.

A practical checklist for international visitors comparing travel eSIMs and other connectivity options for China.

1. Check device compatibility first

Before comparing brands, confirm that your phone supports eSIM and is not carrier-locked. Device compatibility is a hard requirement; an inexpensive plan is useless if the device cannot install it.

2. Install before entering China when possible

Some travel-eSIM providers recommend installing or preparing the eSIM before arrival. This reduces the risk that you need to download an app or retrieve installation information after landing.

3. Decide how much data you really need

  • Light use: maps, messaging, ride-hailing and occasional browsing.
  • Normal use: social media, frequent navigation and short-form video.
  • Heavy use: hotspot, cloud sync, frequent video calls, uploads or remote work.

4. “Unlimited” does not always mean the same thing

Providers may apply daily or total full-speed thresholds before throttling. Always compare the fair-use policy—not only the word “unlimited”.

5. Think about hotspot use

Business travelers and multi-device users should verify tethering or hotspot rules. If you plan to share data with a laptop or another traveler, this can be more important than a small price difference.

6. Compare by your actual trip

A short Beijing–Shanghai city trip may need a different plan from a month-long multi-city trip or a remote-work stay. Our comparison framework therefore starts with trip duration, expected data usage and travel style.

This guide is general travel information. Network conditions, prices, provider policies and local connectivity conditions can change. Always verify the provider's current product terms before purchase.
